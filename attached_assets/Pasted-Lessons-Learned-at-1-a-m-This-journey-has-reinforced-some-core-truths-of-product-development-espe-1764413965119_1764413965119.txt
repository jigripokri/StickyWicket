Lessons Learned at 1 a.m.
This journey has reinforced some core truths of product development, especially in the age of AI.
1. Iterate, Iterate, Iterate: The Scope Emerges as the Product Takes Shape.
My initial spec was for a full storybook builder. But the real, immediate problem was character consistency. Building Characto wasn't a detour; it was a necessary discovery process that unblocked the entire vision. The product roadmap isn't always a straight line. Sometimes, you have to build a bridge before you can cross the river.
2. Gemini as the Ultimate Sidekick.
I’m a PM who codes, but I’m not a full-time engineer. Gemini became my indispensable pair programmer. I used it to brainstorm solutions ("How can I force character consistency across multiple API calls?"), to make sense of dense API reference docs, and to debug cryptic error messages that would have otherwise cost me hours. It didn't write the app for me, but it was the best technical sounding board I could ask for.
3. A Prototype is Not a Production App.
This was my biggest lesson. Passing around and orchestrating dozens of large images is hard. My initial build stored images as base64 strings in the database, a classic prototype move. It fell apart under the slightest load. I had to learn and implement "real" architecture: migrating to dedicated object storage, managing database connection pools to avoid timeouts, and building resilient retry logic. These are the unglamorous but essential details that make a product work.
4. The Key Skills in an AI World: Taste, Agency, and Testing.
When the barrier to creation is lowered, other skills become paramount.
Taste. Is the story good? Is the art compelling? The AI generates options, but a human curator has to apply the taste test.
Agency. The willingness to jump in, get your hands dirty, and learn what is needed to solve a problem is irreplaceable.
Systematic Testing. I learned to prompt the AI to think step-by-step before making a big change, almost like a pre-mortem. ("I'm about to refactor the image generation logic. What are the top 5 things that could break?"). Then, test for those regressions relentlessly.
A Newfound Appreciation for the Craft
More than anything, this project gave me a profound appreciation for the product development cycle we lead every day.
The power of a clear vision. Communicating what you want to an AI is not so different from writing a clear PRD.
The depth of design. I have a newfound respect for the deep thinking our UX, visual, and technical designers bring. Every screen, every step, every choice matters.
The necessity of dogfooding. Using your own product reveals its flaws in a way no metric can.
The importance of distribution. When anyone can create, getting your product in front of the right users is more important than ever.
